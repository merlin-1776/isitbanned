Stephen King's *Different Seasons* is a 1982 collection of four novellas: *Rita Hayworth and Shawshank Redemption*, *Apt Pupil*, *The Body*, and *The Breathing Method*. Two of those stories became *The Shawshank Redemption* and *Stand by Me*, which means a lot of readers know the adaptations without realizing they came from the same book. The collection is less supernatural than much of King's catalog and more interested in the ways ordinary people slide toward obsession, cruelty, or moral compromise.

This title became a genuinely new statewide Utah school ban during the week of July 6-13, 2026. [KUTV reported](https://kutv.com/news/local/stephen-king-novel-different-seasons-banned-from-utah-schools) that Davis, Jordan, Tooele, and Washington school districts had already removed the book, which triggered statewide action under Utah's sensitive-materials law. Utah officials classify the threshold category as "[objective sensitive material](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title53G/Chapter10/53G-10-S103.html)," and the Utah State Board of Education's public statewide removal list was cited in that report as the official record of the change.

## Why You Should Read This

*Different Seasons* is the version of Stephen King that people who don't think they like Stephen King often end up loving. The horror here is mostly human: a prison system built to crush people, a teenager's fascination with Nazi crimes, the terror and tenderness of adolescence, and the stories adults tell to survive what they've lived through. Banning the whole collection means cutting off students from two of the most culturally important coming-of-age and prison narratives of the last half-century, along with the uncomfortable questions King asks in the quieter, darker novellas beside them.
